Overview

This project focuses on the comparative design and analysis of Bell and Aerospike rocket nozzles under identical operating conditions. The study investigates compressible flow behavior and evaluates key performance parameters to understand the advantages and limitations of both nozzle configurations.

The analysis focuses on Mach number, pressure, temperature, thrust, and specific impulse, along with thermal and structural behavior.

Objectives
Design and model Bell and Aerospike rocket nozzles for identical operating conditions.
Analyze compressible flow characteristics using analytical methods and CFD.
Evaluate Mach number, pressure, temperature, thrust, and specific impulse.
Perform thermal and structural analysis to investigate thermal loading, stress, and deformation.
Compare the performance of both nozzle configurations under the selected operating conditions.

Nozzle Configurations
1. Bell Nozzle

The Bell nozzle is a conventional rocket nozzle configuration designed to efficiently expand combustion gases and convert thermal energy into high-velocity exhaust.

Key parameters considered include:

Throat area
Exit area
Area ratio
Chamber conditions
Exit pressure
Mach number
Supersonic expansion
2. Aerospike Nozzle

The Aerospike nozzle uses a central spike instead of a conventional divergent nozzle wall. Its external expansion characteristics allow the exhaust flow to interact with the surrounding atmosphere.

The project investigates its:

Spike geometry
Throat region
Expansion characteristics
Supersonic flow behavior
Pressure distribution
Altitude-compensation characteristics
Analysis Parameters

The two nozzle configurations are compared using:

Parameter	Purpose
Mach Number	Evaluate acceleration and supersonic flow development
